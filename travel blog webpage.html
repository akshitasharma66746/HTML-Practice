Welcome to Bilaspur!

Bilaspur is a scenic and historic hill town in Himachal Pradesh, set along the banks of the Sutlej River. The old name of Bilaspur was Vyaspur which is on the name of rishi vyas. Originally founded in the 7th century as the capital of the princely state of Kahlur, the old town was submerged in the 1950s due to the construction of the iconic Bhakra Dam, giving rise to India’s first planned hill town on higher ground. Today, the district is widely known for the massive Gobind Sagar Lake, the revered Sri Naina Devi Ji Temple, and the ancient Vyaas Gufa, where sage Ved Vyas is believed to have meditated.

Famous Tourist Attractions:

Bandla Dhar

Bandla Dhar is a serene, high-altitude hilltop located in the Bilaspur district of Himachal Pradesh. Perched at an elevation of roughly 1,350 meters above sea level, this scenic destination is surrounded by lush pine forests and offers sweeping, 360-degree panoramic views of the Bilaspur valley, the Dhauladhar mountain range, and the shimmering waters of Gobind Sagar Lake. Beyond its quiet natural beauty and winding mountain drive, Bandla Dhar is widely celebrated as a premier paragliding hub, where flyers take off from the grassy ridge and land along the banks of the reservoir at Luhnu Ground. Whether visiting for adventure sports, nature trails, or a peaceful sunset getaway, it stands out as one of Bilaspur's most picturesque hidden gems

Rukmani Kund

Rukmani Kund is a serene, sacred water reservoir nestled amidst the forested Shivalik Hills near Auhar in the Bilaspur district of Himachal Pradesh. Steeped in poignant local folklore, the pool is named after Rukmani, a young newlywed woman who is said to have sacrificed her life here to end a severe drought and bring perpetual water to the parched region. Today, the crystal-clear, never-drying reservoir serves as both a vital water source for surrounding villages and a peaceful spiritual retreat featuring a small shrine. Visitors and locals pay tribute to her memory by tying colorful ribbons and bangles to the hanging grass along its rocky walls, which is locally revered as a symbol of her presence.

Baba Nahar Singh Temple

Dholra Temple, officially known as the Baba Nahar Singh Ji Temple, is a revered Hindu shrine perched on a hillside along the banks of Gobind Sagar Lake in Bilaspur, Himachal Pradesh. Built in the 17th century by Raja Deep Chand after his Kullu-born queen experienced mysterious fainting spells, the temple honors Baba Nahar Singh—also locally called Bajia or Peepal Wala—who is believed to have accompanied her from Naggar. Unlike conventional temples with stone idols, this sacred shrine houses and worships the deity's wooden slippers (kharaun). Overlooking the tranquil reservoir, the temple draws thousands of devotees seeking divine justice and blessings, especially during the vibrant fairs held every Tuesday in the Hindu month of Jeth.
